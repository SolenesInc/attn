## A. Shadowed wall

Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: static pixel-art side-view surfing game visual prototype, OPTION A: SHADOWED WALL. A single landscape 16:9 frame, no UI, no lettering.
Primary request: Explore a wave barrel that looks like a long mass of moving water with a deep shaded rideable space underneath. It must look like an actual low-resolution indie pixel game screenshot, not a painting, illustration poster or 3D render.
Scene: clean powder-blue sky in upper 43%, one small stepped dark navy/green island on far left horizon; turquoise ocean, underwater sandy seabed cross-section in bottom 15%, a few tiny pixel fish below surface. Daylight, quiet and spacious.
Composition: side elevation, mostly parallel to wave travel with a very slight oblique look into the hollow. One long wave occupies the right 75% of frame. Its thick, almost horizontal crest sits at 43% frame height, with a small overhanging lip at x=28%. Water face extends across to the right frame edge, base at 79% frame height. The opening is LONG and HORIZONTAL, NOT a round tube viewed end-on.
Surfer: one tiny dark-haired pixel surfer in muted coral short-sleeve shirt and dark blue shorts on a cream board, at x=56%, board y=76%. Surfer height 7% of frame, side-facing left, knees slightly bent, arms balancing. Keep surfer clearly small relative to wave.
OPTION A treatment: A broad blue-green water WALL behind the surfer. Beneath the overhanging crest, a long irregular dark cobalt/navy shadow spreads from x=32% to x=80%, about three times wider than it is tall. The shadow feels part of the water mass, not an outlined hole. A shallow hooked lip on the LEFT and the shadow under the thick roof suggest a barrel; surfer is fully readable inside shadow. Right-hand face sweeps upward into the continuous crest. Broken mint-white streaks curve diagonally down its face and converge into foam near the board; sparse flecks, broad solid blocks of blue remain visible. Thin sunlit mint crest edge, heavy blue shadow underneath, teal floor reflection.
Pixel style: a believable 400x225 game framebuffer enlarged with NEAREST NEIGHBOR, square pixels, simple deliberate clusters, about 14 flat colors, coarse stair-stepped contours and pixel sprites. Crisp flat contiguous color regions. Reference mood is Surf Sandbox-style side-on pixel surfing, but create an original scene. No antialiased curves, smooth gradients, tiny noisy dithering, rings, concentric arches, geometric rainbow ribbons, tunnel view, photoreal surf photography, lettering, frames or HUD.

## B. Open hollow

Use case: precise-object-edit
Asset type: OPTION B, static pixel-art surfing game barrel study, OPEN HOLLOW CURL.
Input image 1 is the base scene / edit target. Change ONLY the shape and shading of the large wave. Preserve the entire sky, horizon, island, underwater floor, fish, surfer appearance, surfer pose and screen position, board, pixel-grid size, limited palette, and framing exactly.
Make this a clearly different barrel treatment: its opening has a rounded C-shaped lip in side/three-quarter view, an overhanging roof curves FORWARD toward the camera and down on the left, and the curved inside wall makes a very long vaulted room around the surfer. Build one heavy continuous translucent-turquoise water mass from the right edge over the surfer to the breaking lip at x=30%. Give the opening a large softly stepped navy shadow in the back, and a thin bright turquoise inner reflected-light rim ONLY at the lower left, so depth comes from the hollow's curved volume, not a flat horizontal stripe. The opening is wide and horizontal, 2.5 times wider than tall, occupying about x=28%..80% and y=46%..76%; surfer remains clearly inside the lower-right half of the barrel with empty headroom above.
Show near lip in front and rear wall behind as visibly separate overlapping surfaces. The descending near lip stops well LEFT of the surfer, so the surfer is unobscured. Increase the roof's curved overhang and volume compared with the base image, do not just recolor the same rectangle. Broken thin mint foam traces sweep over the roof and down the right face, a little white spray on the left landing lip. Broad clean flat pixel colors, coarse pixel clusters, no smooth painterly treatment.
Avoid circular tunnels viewed end-on, multiple concentric rings, rainbow bands, literal outlines around the shadow, photorealism, new scenery, new characters, text, labels or UI. Single landscape frame, preserve image dimensions.

## C. Water curtain

Use case: precise-object-edit
Asset type: OPTION C, static pixel-art surfing game barrel study, TRANSLUCENT FOREGROUND CURTAIN.
Input image 1 is the base scene / edit target. Change ONLY the large wave and its interaction with visibility of the surfer. Preserve sky, horizon, island, underwater floor and fish, surfer's appearance, pose and screen position, board, pixel-grid size, palette and framing.
Make this a visibly different treatment: keep a long heavy near-horizontal water crest extending to the right screen edge, but bring a thin glassy turquoise sheet of water DOWN IN FRONT OF the surfer. The near curtain sweeps diagonally from x=39%, y=50% to x=66%, y=75%, partially covering the surfer at x=54%, y=66%..73%. This transparent waterfall is the front wall of the barrel, not the back wall. The dark cobalt long cavern and far water face remain BEHIND the surfer. Through the overlapping front sheet, the coral shirt is still readable but muted dark teal and the board is faintly cream; clear parts of the body remain visible through gaps. Deliberately partially occlude him so his position INSIDE the wave is unmistakable. The curtain has a few curving pale-green vertical highlights, sparse broken ivory foam streaks, a ragged lower edge, and holes/glassy patches, not a solid blue rectangle. Strong cast shadow under the crest, turquoise reflected water at the floor. Breaking spray collects where the near curtain meets the trough, leaving the wave to the right mostly solid teal with a few sloping foam tracks.
This is a 2D side-view game rendering approach: rear body, surfer, translucent near sheet. It must read as a long hollow, NOT an end-on circular tube. Use deliberate coarse flat pixel clusters, no airbrush or smooth translucent photo gradients. Do not simply put a uniformly opaque stripe over the surfer.
Constraints: only one surfer, identical position, no change to the background or underwater area, no extra objects, no lettering, labels or HUD. Preserve image dimensions.
