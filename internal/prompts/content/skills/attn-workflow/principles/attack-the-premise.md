# Attack the premise

Apply when two fixes that share one assumption have failed the same check, or fixes keep arriving in the same shape.

When two fixes that share one assumption have failed the same check, write the assumption down and test it directly before writing a third. Each failed fix is evidence against the belief it rested on. Find an observation that would come out differently if the assumption were false, and make it, with a check you can rerun, as [Build the lever](build-the-lever.md) describes. Look at how the failures are distributed, not only at how large they are. If the evidence contradicts the assumption, the cause is elsewhere: look for what the failures have in common that the assumption did not predict. If it confirms the assumption, ask the next why, as [Fix root causes](fix-root-causes.md) describes: the earlier fixes may have treated a consequence instead of the cause. Remove the condition instead of compensating for it: if one part always lands on the losing side, change what puts it there, because a mechanism that cleans up after it leaves the cause in place and adds work on every run. The design is the assumption when fixes pass but keep arriving in the same shape: special cases for unrelated edge cases, casts or optional fields that are always set, a lock where the design said nothing was shared, callers that must know the callee's internal rules. Stop patching. [Redesign](redesign-around-the-new-requirement.md) as if the new constraint had been known from the start, aiming smaller than the patched design, and bring the redesign to the coordinating agent named in the brief, or to the user when none is named, before more patches land.
